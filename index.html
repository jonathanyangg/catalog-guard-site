Why now

The duty-free de minimis exemption ended globally in August 2025. Since then the rules have kept moving: a Supreme Court decision in February 2026, IEEPA duty refunds opening in April 2026, and new Section 338 tariffs on certain Canada-origin goods from August 22, 2026, applied by HTS subheading.

Shopify's own guidance to merchants: keep the HS codes and country of origin on your products accurate. Wrong or missing codes mean wrong duties at checkout, held shipments, and avoidable penalties.

How it works

  1. Read-only scan. Catalog Guard reviews every product and variant for missing HS codes, missing origin, and codes that do not match what the product actually is.
  2. Evidence-backed suggestions. Each proposed fix comes with the reasoning and source behind it. Where origin cannot be determined from your data, it is reported as unknown, never guessed.
  3. You approve every change. Nothing writes to your store until you approve it, line by line or in bulk. Every write is logged.
  4. It keeps watching. New products get checked as you add them, and your catalog is re-reviewed when tariff rules change.

What you get

  • A clean bill of health, or a fix list. A per-product report: compliant, missing data, or likely wrong code with the proposed correction.
  • An audit trail. Every suggestion, approval, and write, exportable for your broker or accountant.
  • Rule-change alerts. When a change like Section 338 lands, you hear which of your products it touches, in plain language.

Built on a simple rule: customs data has consequences, so a person approves every change. The software does the reading; you do the deciding.
